# Organon Rationale

This non-normative companion explains the relationships, grounds, and limits of the [English philosophical authority](../PHILOSOPHY.md). It does not add obligations to the philosophy.

Sections 1–5 explain the current structure, its reasons, and its cases. Sections 6–7 distinguish philosophical constraints from contextual application choices. These examples illustrate judgments, not a mandatory workflow or proof of universal correctness.

## 1. Placement and Changes in Normative Status

| Previous content | Current placement | Change and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generative Drive | Renamed Self-Transcendence in the charter; its quoted provision is unchanged. | Generative orientation and non-finality are explained separately from grounds for achievement. The new name adds no requirement of autonomous execution, inevitable progress, or change on every action. |
| Internal Consistency and Reflexivity | Charter, unchanged. | Their distinct requirements remain; neither a new name nor a shorter structure establishes their correctness. |
| Grounds: articulation, assessment, proportionate support, and evidence boundaries | Grounds, §§2.1–2.2. | General requirements and limits retain their normative force. The Harness example appears below without making any assessment framework mandatory. |
| Method Capabilities: uniform definitions of mastery and generation | Withdrawn as a core standard; capability claims fall under Grounds, §2.3. | The previous requirement that a system understand and explain method formation to qualify as capable of method generation no longer applies universally. Applications can still adopt it for stated reasons. This changes the former standard rather than merely renaming it. |
| Openness to Alternative Implementations | Grounds, §2.4, as an additional choice commitment. | The provision and its conditions remain. Its placement does not make it a deduction from general support requirements. |
| Repeated summaries in overview, meanings, terminology, and relationships | Definitions remain with their provisions; relationships explain distinctions and mutual constraints. | Necessary meanings and limits remain in the core. Removing duplicate summaries does not withdraw a commitment. |

## 2. Reasons for Adoption and Their Limits

### 2.1 Commitments and Objections

The following gives reasons for adopting the commitments under Organon's chosen aim of a cross-system philosophy of generation and judgment. These are arguments for that position, not proofs of universal necessity or additions to the core.

| Commitment | Reason for adoption and premise | Objection and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-Transcendence | Valuing broader understanding and constructive possibilities gives a reason not to treat present forms as final. This takes their expansion as worth pursuing, rather than treating permission to change as sufficient. | Expansion can be costly or conflict with other values. The commitment does not rank it above every competing objective or require change in every action. Its worth is an adopted value, not established merely by a system's growth. |
| Internal Consistency | If judgments are to guide assessment and construction together, requiring incompatible conclusions under identical conditions undermines that joint guidance. This supports examining their compatibility. | A system may encounter unresolved tensions or conflicting evidence. Distinguishing conditions, qualifying claims, or withdrawing a judgment can address these; consistency alone neither selects the correct judgment nor proves its assumptions. |
| Reflexivity | A system's methods and principles can contribute to the errors it attributes to other objects. Applying relevant requirements to them makes those sources open to correction rather than exempting them because they are its own. | Self-assessment can be biased and cannot establish its own sufficiency. Reflexivity retains applicability conditions and does not demand an endless review chain or a particular independent-review mechanism. |
| Grounds | When judgments guide understanding and construction, identifiable reasons allow their support and limits to be challenged. Proportionality restrains claims that go beyond those reasons. | Articulating grounds can be incomplete or costly. Different claims call for different assessment, not a common numerical test. Initial value commitments need not prove every starting premise, but declaring them does not settle criticism. |
| Openness to Alternative Implementations | If choices are to serve stated objectives, values, and constraints, identity or status alone leaves their relevance to that choice unexplained. This motivates retaining the additional restriction on sufficient reasons. | Continuity, tradition, and identity can matter to an explicitly adopted value. They are not disallowed; the connection, limits, and trade-offs still need assessment. This position does not follow merely from the ability to compare performance. |

These reasons remain open to criticism under Grounds. Identifying a role for each commitment does not prove that the collection is complete, uniquely organized, or obligatory for every system. Applications supply further objectives and values; this philosophy does not specify a universal ordering of them.

### 2.2 Capability Standards in Applications

The former core defined method generation capacity to require understanding and explaining the grounds and limits of method formation or revision. The current core withdraws that uniform definition. Reliable production, understanding, and explanation can be assessed as distinct claims, without prescribing one capability hierarchy for every system.

External evidence can support a specified capability even when the system cannot explain its internal process. Conversely, reliable output alone need not support a claim to understand that process. An application may require such understanding and explanation, but must make that requirement explicit rather than attributing it to the core. Revising a definition changes the claim assessed; it cannot retroactively establish the former claim.

This repository retains explanation of method revisions and assessment under relevant variations because its chosen methods should make proposed changes reviewable. That reason supports a contextual method requirement, not a universal test of generation capability. A relevant variation retains the method's application conditions; it is not an arbitrary demand for universal transfer. Forming new methods and revising existing ones can both be assessed, without requiring additional levels of abstraction or a fixed document format as evidence of capability.

### 2.3 Assessment Tools

Measurement can make some differences comparable, repeated assessment can examine stability, and a Harness can organize assessment conditions. For example, a software Harness may run update–read sequences under specified conditions, while a research team may compare interpretations of interview accounts. Each choice needs contextual reasons. These examples do not alter the core's limits: the three are not a universally necessary chain, an unreproduced observation can offer limited support, and random outcomes need not be identical on every occasion.

### 2.4 Grounds for the Analysis Method

The [principled-review skill](../skills/organon-core-principled-review/SKILL.md) makes the selected review responsibilities explicit and keeps their definitions in one place. Its purpose is to expose consequential omissions, unsupported claims, and alternatives that a less structured review might miss. That purpose is a reason for trying and assessing the method, not evidence that six dimensions are sufficient for every task. The method itself remains open to criticism under the core, including criticism of its coverage and cost. Its findings identify their standards: an object’s commitments, a selected comparison basis, and the constraints on the review method are distinct. Applying Organon in the review does not establish that the object has adopted it; a method preference does not by itself establish a core conflict. Ordinary narrow assessments and wording corrections need not acquire the full review burden.

This method adopts a conditional preference for parsimony, not a claim that simplicity establishes truth. The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy discussion of simplicity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simplicity/) distinguishes the complexity of principles or hypotheses from the kinds of entities postulated; reducing one can increase the other. It also distinguishes reasons to adopt a working theory from reasons to believe it more likely true. These distinctions motivate identifying the comparison and its relevant constraints rather than treating shorter wording or fewer terms as decisive. The adopted review remains a contextual method choice, not an additional core obligation or proof that the less complex candidate is true.

## 3. Removal Check

| Provision temporarily removed | Explicit requirement removed and its distinction from the others |
| --- | --- |
| Self-Transcendence | The others can constrain judgments, capability claims, and implementation choices without independently requiring an intrinsic orientation toward expanding generative possibilities. Requiring support for capability claims does not itself require valuing capability expansion. |
| Internal Consistency | There is no longer a separate requirement that simultaneously held judgments be compatible under the same conditions. Assessing support relations must be distinguished from the joint compatibility of judgments. Separate reasons for each judgment do not by themselves establish overall consistency. |
| Reflexivity | Grounds already concerns principles and judgments, but does not independently specify that relevant principles of generation also govern their own formation and revision. Removing Reflexivity loses that full self-application requirement, without eliminating every element of self-assessment. |
| Grounds | The charter retains self-assessment and requirements for grounds of improvement, but does not independently specify articulability, appropriate assessment for different kinds of claims, and proportionality of support to claim strength and scope. |
| Openness to Alternative Implementations, the choice provision within Grounds | General reasons for a choice can still be assessed, but the explicit commitment that name, convention, or established status alone is insufficient to establish priority is lost. |

The removal check distinguishes contributions and overlaps; it does not claim to establish independence in formal logic. A “loss” means the requirement is no longer explicitly supplied by that provision, not that it could never be derived from other content under any additional assumptions.

## 4. Illustrative Cases

The following cases distinguish the current requirements and the application-specific capability standards. These examine the meaning of the philosophy; they are not steps a system is required to execute.

| Case | Judgment and limits |
| --- | --- |
| A system has grounds to retain its methods while maintaining an orientation toward understanding and changing them. | Stability does not directly violate self-transcendence. Maintaining the orientation does not establish that actual capability has grown. |
| A system revises a principle and withdraws its previous judgment, so that it no longer holds the two contradictory judgments simultaneously. | Changing a view does not directly violate internal consistency. Whether the revision has adequate grounds remains to be assessed. |
| A system’s implications are mutually consistent but depend on a false assumption. | It may remain internally consistent without thereby being correct. |
| Two evaluations emphasize speed and quality respectively, without yet requiring incompatible conclusions. | Tension between values does not itself constitute a contradiction. Actual incompatibility must be assessed under the same conditions and meanings. |
| Under the same assumptions, meanings, and scope, a system simultaneously asserts that a judgment on the same question holds and does not hold. | This violates internal consistency. Describing the system as undergoing self-transcendence does not remove the contradiction. |
| A system assesses its own principles and produces new principles on that basis. | This addresses reflexivity’s requirement concerning its objects, but does not thereby prove the old or new principles correct, or automatically establish the adequacy of the revision. |
| Grounds are fully stated, but an inference merely fails to conflict with its assumptions rather than being supported by them. | Articulability and compatibility do not establish the inference. Grounds requires examination of the support relation. |
| A system explicitly states a value starting point as a commitment and explains its reasons, limits, and consequences. | It can be assessed as a value commitment. Lack of numerical results does not directly refute it, nor can it be presented as an empirical necessity. |
| A failure that has not yet been reproduced has a verifiable record. | It may provide corresponding limited support. Whether it supports a particular judgment depends on the record, claim, and scope; it cannot be categorically excluded or generalized without limits. |
| A random system produces different outputs each time but exhibits stable characteristics under stated assessment conditions. | Nonidentical outputs do not automatically invalidate evidence. The conditions and stable characteristics must be examined for what conclusions they support. |
| An application distinguishes using existing methods from forming or revising them. A system can explain and apply existing methods under relevant variations, but has no evidence of forming or revising methods. | The evidence supports the specified use capability, not automatically the separate formation or revision claim. These capability definitions come from the application. |
| An application defines generation capability as producing usable new methods in a stated task family. External evidence supports that capability, but the system cannot explain its internal process. | Lack of internal explanation does not itself disqualify the specified capability. The evidence must support the claimed scope and does not automatically establish understanding of the process. |
| An application explicitly requires a system to understand and explain the grounds and limits of forming methods; the same reliable black box cannot do so. | Reliable production does not satisfy that application requirement. Removing the former uniform core definition does not remove an explicitly chosen contextual requirement. |
| After failing a capability criterion, an author narrows its definition and says the original claim passed unchanged. | A revised definition can support a new assessment, but cannot conceal the change or establish the earlier claim. |
| A system cites one hundred near-identical method documents as its only evidence of expanded understanding and constructive capability. | The count supports a quantity claim, not automatically the claimed expansion. Capability definitions and relevant evidence are still required. |
| An existing approach receives priority because of explicit compatibility or maintenance conditions. | Conventional use is not the sole ground. Relevant conditions can provide reasons for the choice, subject to assessment appropriate to their nature. |
| An approach receives priority solely from its name, conventional use, or established status, without relevant reasons. | Openness to Alternative Implementations treats these grounds as insufficient. It does not thereby require an unconditional preference for a new approach. |
| Two implementations currently give the same answers, but method capabilities, costs, or other relevant effects have not been included in the comparison. | This supports only a comparison within the stated scope, not overall equivalence. A difference not yet assessed must be distinguished from the absence of a difference. |
| A workshop values the continuation of a traditional instrument-making practice and explains how using and teaching its methods serves that objective, within stated resource limits and with acknowledged trade-offs. | Preserving the practice can be an explicitly adopted starting value. Its connection to the implementation can provide a reason for choosing traditional methods, not merely their established status. Whether a particular choice serves that value and fits the constraints still requires assessment; declaring the objective does not give every traditional implementation priority. |
| An approach was preferred only because “it has always been done this way.” That phrase is renamed “the objective,” without explaining the value preserved, the limits of application, or the trade-offs. | Renaming alone adds no support. Grounds still calls for the relevant explanation and assessment; this does not reject tradition or preference as a starting value, or demand an endless chain of external justification. |

## 5. Applying the Philosophy to This Reconstruction

Adding principles is not an improvement merely because the count increases. Grounds distinguishes articulation from assessment and limits the support claimed. Capability claims now require application-specific meanings and proportionate support rather than the former uniform definitions. Openness to Alternative Implementations makes explicit that identity or status cannot establish priority on its own. These contributions and their limits can be re-examined through the relationships and cases above.

Assessment of this text likewise distinguishes philosophical commitments, inferences, and empirical claims. Making a commitment explicit provides no self-proof. Identifying a distinct contribution does not prove universal necessity. Finding no conflict does not amount to proving correctness or independence.

Checks of confirmed wording, corresponding sections, links, and file hashes verify documentary consistency and the scope of changes; they cannot replace philosophical argument. These checks do not confer philosophical authority on tools, governance procedures, or particular implementations.

## 6. Constraints Across Different Systems

The following are hypothetical applications. Their contextual assumptions supply premises beyond the philosophy; their conclusions hold within those premises.

### 6.1 Research Team

| Argument element | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | A team can improve its ability to explain why participants abandon a service by revising how it classifies interview accounts. |
| Additional premises | The team values explanatory understanding, wants its classification revisions to be reviewable, and treats participants’ accounts as situated evidence. It has consented interview material, limited staff time, and an existing classification that assigns one reason to each account. The intended claim concerns the interviewed participants; the material does not establish population prevalence. |
| Inference | Self-Transcendence supports openness to revising the classification without demanding novelty. Grounds requires separating what participants reported from the team’s inference and limiting generalization. Internal Consistency requires resolving incompatible classifications under the same definitions or explaining a change of definition. Reflexivity extends this scrutiny to the classification method and its revision. The team distinguishes competent coding from the ability to revise its method, and its stated reviewability objective requires explaining the reasons for that revision. |
| Counterexample | An account describes both cost and accessibility barriers and explicitly explains how they interact. A single-label method loses this reported relationship; adding labels without explaining what they distinguish may merely increase vocabulary. A revised method permits multiple reasons and records their stated relationship. To assess the revision, the team compares what each method preserves on this account and on other relevant accounts, including ones with only one stated reason. Agreement among coders does not by itself establish that the explanation is correct. |
| Conclusion | The revision has a specific potential contribution: preserving relationships reported in the account that the original classification omitted. Preserving those reports does not establish that the relationships hold causally in the world. An explanation and assessment on relevant variations can support a bounded claim that the team improved its classification method under its stated objective; the number of labels alone cannot. Under Openness to Alternative Implementations, joint discussion may suit a small exploratory team, while separate initial readings may help examine shared interpretive bias. These approaches have different costs and evidential limits; either choice needs reasons tied to the study. Neither a software Harness nor an agent skill follows necessarily from this philosophy. |

### 6.2 Software System

| Argument element | Application |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | A service team can improve its method for evaluating two read implementations by including changes to the underlying data. |
| Additional premises | The service must return the latest acknowledged update on subsequent reads and meet a stated latency budget. The team also requires evaluation-method revisions to be explainable and tested under relevant variations so reviewers can assess their contribution. The candidates are direct reads and cached reads. The current comparison uses unchanged data and reports matching answers and acceptable latency; it has not examined cache invalidation after an update. These are application requirements, not requirements imposed by Organon on every service. |
| Inference | Grounds limits the existing comparison to unchanged data. Internal Consistency prevents simultaneously claiming the specified freshness guarantee and accepting stale reads under that same guarantee. Self-Transcendence supports improving the evaluation method when its scope misses a relevant condition. Reflexivity requires assessing the method’s own coverage claim. The team’s reviewability requirement calls for explaining why an update followed by a read is a relevant variation and how adding it changes the support available; this is an application premise, not a universal core capability standard. |
| Counterexample | Both candidates pass the original comparison, but one returns a cached value after an acknowledged update. Matching local answers did not establish overall equivalence. The revised method compares both candidates on update–read sequences and retains unchanged-data cases. Passing those cases still leaves unexamined failures, concurrency conditions, or workloads outside their scope. |
| Conclusion | The revision can expose a specific omission and support a capability claim bounded by the cases assessed, when the revised method’s use and limits are understood. Openness to Alternative Implementations permits direct reads for simpler freshness reasoning or cached reads with adequately supported invalidation when latency conditions justify them. Their trade-offs are not erased by a matching output. Executable tests may be useful here, while the research team may use interpretive comparison; the philosophy does not require identical methods or make this text repository a software test Harness. |

The common constraint concerns reasons, scope, consistency, support for capability claims, and self-application. The differences concern what counts as relevant evidence and which methods serve the stated objectives. These examples show applicability in two contexts, not completeness across all systems.

## 7. Grounds and Limits of This Repository’s Form

The repository distinguishes three roles: the core states philosophical commitments; this rationale explains arguments and limits; skills offer revisable application methods. A skill instruction does not become a philosophical obligation merely by referring to the core.

| Chosen implementation | Contextual reason | Limit and possible alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English authority with a corresponding Chinese version | The agreed arrangement provides a single reference for resolving translation differences while supporting Chinese reading. | No principle establishes English as intrinsically preferable. An inaccurate English statement needs explicit correction; its authority does not establish its truth. Another project could select a different language. |
| A text-centered core and script-free skills | The intended library centers on articulable commitments and a thin agent-facing layer. Text makes the reasons and instructions directly inspectable without maintaining an execution framework. | Text alone does not establish clarity or capability. Another application may have grounds for scripts or other tools; their absence here is a design choice. |
| `organon-core-assess` and `organon-core-absorb` | Separate entrypoints distinguish current philosophical compatibility from reasons for philosophical adoption. Absorb coordinates feedback, review, the user’s decision, and necessary synchronization. | Compatibility alone does not justify adoption; conflict alone does not refute revision reasons. Independent skill and documentation maintenance belongs to the shared workflow, not absorption. |
| `organon-core-principled-review` | A shared six-dimension review supplies grounded findings to assess and absorb and can be used independently. Its name identifies a method rather than limiting the object to the system itself. | Findings retain their standards and limits; they neither automatically establish a philosophical conflict nor decide adoption. Reuse does not replace an independent initial judgment or require recursive review. |
| External `grill-me/grilling` | Reuse of the selected questioning method supports explicit grilling requests and material unresolved choices after facts have been gathered. | A fully specified ordinary assessment need not load it. Missing capability leaves the dependent stage incomplete; unrelated checks can continue. Neither its name nor its questions have philosophical authority. |
| An independent agent for cross-review, with an initial judgment before seeing the author’s proposal | A separate initial assessment is intended to expose omissions without immediately adopting the author’s framing. This is an agreed review method, not a deduction from Reflexivity. | Separate agents may share assumptions and errors. A consensus is not proof. A human reviewer or another review structure may be appropriate elsewhere; an unavailable independent agent means this chosen cross-review remains incomplete. |
| Explanation of method revisions and assessment under relevant variations | This repository seeks reviewable changes: explaining the revision and examining a relevant variation helps assess the claimed contribution beyond a new text. | These are contextual method requirements. A completed explanation or test does not establish a universal capability level or prove improvement outside the assessed scope. |
| A bounded iteration record and a fixed baseline within each assessment | Explicit premises, differences, and unresolved issues help track support and prevent a candidate revision from silently changing the standard used to judge it. | A completed record is not evidence of improvement by itself. Changes to the philosophy require the user’s explicit decision in this repository; that allocation of authority is a contextual arrangement, not a philosophical theorem. |

Self-application therefore includes examining these methods and their claimed benefits. A useful method revision needs an identifiable contribution, reasons, and assessment under relevant variations. Repeated agreement without new support does not make a claim stronger; lack of support or unresolved disagreement can justify ending an iteration with a limited conclusion. This avoids treating an ever-longer review chain as proof.

The repository’s philosophical character is supported when its commitments change substantive judgments, constrain its own claims, and guide reasoned choices across different systems. A directory structure, a set of skills, and a review loop do not establish that character on their own. The [skill validation cases](../skills/references/validation.md) examine the selected application methods; their results cannot prove the philosophy universally correct.
