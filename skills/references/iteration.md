# Iteration and cross-validation

Use this record for designing, implementing, assessing, or revising an Organon skill or repository text, and for a caller's selected philosophical adoption. It organizes inspectable grounds; filling it out is not proof. [Philosophy resolution](philosophy-resolution.md) fixes the adopted baseline for the current operation. The bundled [Core](../../PHILOSOPHY.md) supplies this method's philosophical constraints and remains the baseline for Core repository maintenance; that link does not override a downstream caller's selected text. This method implements the repository's chosen objectives and constraints. Explaining method revisions and testing relevant variations serve this repository's reviewability objectives; they do not impose a uniform capability definition on every assessed system. The initiating agent reads [validation](validation.md) to select cases before a method change. Independent test agents must record their initial responses before seeing the expected boundaries.

## Six-part record

1. **Object and baseline.** Identify the input, the selected adopted philosophy and its path, the adoption target, and relevant text versions or snapshots. Keep the baseline fixed within an assessment and pass it to delegated skills and reviewers. Distinguish a proposed replacement from the baseline it challenges and the Core method's constraints from the selected philosophy's commitments.
2. **Problem and objective.** State the decision to resolve, relevant conditions, and what would count as a useful improvement. Stability or no absorption may be justified.
3. **Grounds and additional premises.** Cite applicable provisions; distinguish observations, inferences, value commitments, and application choices. State support limits and explain non-applicability when material. Do not force every provision onto every input.
4. **Candidates and trade-offs.** Compare the meaningful alternatives, including retaining the current form. Explain the destination and distinctive contribution of any absorption. Distinguish a minimal correction to an input from a change to the selected philosophy or its repository.
5. **Assessment and counterexamples.** State expected boundaries before implementation, examine actual responses and relevant variations, and record self-assessment and independent review separately. Assess outputs, the method, and its revision criteria. Explain failures and the grounds and limits of method formation or revision.
6. **Decision and remaining questions.** State the conclusion, applicable authorization, changes made or still proposed, and remaining uncertainty. For another round, identify what new grounds or justified revision warrant continuing, and preserve the preceding judgment and material differences.

Use concise prose; ordinary assessments need not expand into six verbose sections. Repository changes use an inspectable record under `.local/iterations/`; ordinary input assessments may remain in the conversation. Only selected, authorized, general arguments or cases enter maintained documentation. Do not place private inputs in public examples.

## Full review and reuse

[organon-core-principled-review](../organon-core-principled-review/SKILL.md) is the shared source for full-analysis triggers and the six-dimension method. It distinguishes the object’s commitments, the selected review standards, and the method’s own constraints. Assess owns current philosophical compatibility judgments; absorb owns the case for philosophical adoption. Both reuse findings with their grounds and standards when applicable; targeted assessments and wording-only tasks do not acquire a full-analysis requirement merely by using this record. The principled-review skill returns findings to its caller without invoking assess or absorb. Its examination of its report, method, and criteria remains within one bounded review and does not replace the caller's independent cross-validation.

Criticizing criteria does not change the fixed baseline of the current assessment. A proposed change forms a new version and requires a separate judgment, retaining the prior result and reasons for the difference. The authority and stopping rules below also apply to principled review.

## Repository maintenance

The initiating agent owns independent skill, rationale, translation, and other documentation maintenance under [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); it is not philosophical absorption merely because repository text changes. Use assess for current philosophical compatibility, principled review under its stated triggers, and [wording review](../organon-core-wording-review/SKILL.md) for wording. If maintenance reveals a proposed change to a core commitment, meaning, or applicability, identify it and use absorb when the task calls for that decision. Do not silently extend the authorized scope.

Before editing, preserve the relevant current worktree baseline and original requirements, including existing user changes. State acceptance cases and obtain independent initial assessment before disclosing the author’s candidate. Retain direct checks of task requirements, scope and authorization, grounds, and actual validation evidence. Review wording separately from philosophical judgments; apply [translation guidance](../../zh/AGENTS.md) to bilingual correspondence. Synchronize affected documents and links without importing private inputs into maintained examples.

For method changes, explain the formation or revision and apply the candidate to a relevant variation. Give independent test agents raw cases without the expected boundaries, preserve their actual responses, then compare. These are contextual requirements for reviewable method changes, not universal capability definitions. File checks and planned tests do not establish actual behavior or improvement. Keep local evidence of checks performed, failures, narrow corrections, and reruns; repeat checks only when material changes or unresolved failures justify them. Wording-only maintenance retains these applicable checks without acquiring a full six-dimension review.

After required cross-review, apply changes within existing authorization and verify the actual result against the reviewed proposal. Preserve findings for the output, method, and criteria within one bounded self-check; do not create a recursive review chain. The authority, record, and stopping rules apply to maintenance as well as absorption.

## Independent cross-validation

The initiating agent owns the iteration. Cross-validation requires a different agent, not the initiator adopting a second role. Give that agent the raw input, fixed philosophical baseline, objectives, and necessary contextual facts first. Withhold the author's candidate, rationale, and expected verdicts until the reviewer records its own assessment. Then share them and request comparison, counterexamples, and a reasoned account of any disagreement. If reviewing an existing skill as the object, its text is raw input; withhold the new proposed revision and the author's verdict.

For an absorption candidate, independently examine both its relationship to the original philosophy using `organon-core-assess` and the grounds for revising that philosophy, using `organon-core-principled-review` when applicable. Compatibility is not sufficient for adoption, and conflict alone does not defeat revision reasons. For independent repository maintenance, review the proposed method or text through the maintenance workflow above rather than broadening absorb. Ordinary assessment does not automatically require absorption. A delegated reviewer returns findings to the initiator rather than spawning another review chain. Subsequent reviews may reuse the recorded independent first judgment and must distinguish new evidence from information learned through comparison.

If an independent reviewer is unavailable, retain a provisional result and state that cross-validation is incomplete. Do not certify a candidate as cross-validated or adopt it through this method before that review. Different agents may share blind spots, and consensus cannot replace support. The user decides unresolved material trade-offs; do not silently choose a different philosophical commitment to secure agreement.

## Dependency, authority, and stopping

Use the externally available `grilling` capability when explicitly requested or when material unresolved choices require user input, directly or through the `grill-me` entrypoint. Gather discoverable facts first. Resolve the capability by name and read its actual instructions, following the entrypoint to its target. Do not hard-code a machine path, silently install it, or replace it with an in-repository imitation. Without an explicit request or a material unresolved choice, no dependency lookup or interview is required. If a required capability is unavailable, report it and pause the requested grilling or dependent decision stage; continue independent checks and fact gathering. Missing grilling does not supply answers or prevent a fully specified ordinary assessment.

Within these skills, apply grilling's design tree to unresolved choices that materially affect the current task. Do not ask users to supply discoverable facts, reopen settled choices, or require a separate final confirmation merely because grilling was invoked. Once those choices are resolved, continue only within existing authorization and after other prerequisites, including required cross-validation, are satisfied. An unanswered material choice remains unresolved; stopping an iteration does not supply an answer or authorization. File-change classifications and format checks do not replace the reasons for philosophical adoption, independent review, or the user's decision on the concrete candidate.

Default to a concrete proposal. Existing explicit authorization permits changes within its scope; do not require repeated approval for the same authorized work. Changes to philosophical meaning need the user's explicit decision, including changes hidden in definitions, translations, or examples. A candidate does not become the baseline merely because an agent generated or approved it. If English and Chinese disagree, follow the English authority; do not silently reinterpret the English text to reconcile them.

Stop when the relevant question is resolved, or when no new grounds or justified revision warrant continuing. New grounds may include a better argument, a counterexample, or a clarified premise, not only new observations. Report unresolved disagreement or missing support without declaring success. Progress is not the number of rounds, documents, or accepted additions; a revision needs a discernible contribution under the stated objectives.

For maintained Lean evidence, also follow [Lean evidence maintenance](../../AGENTS.md#lean-evidence-maintenance): actual dependency changes determine the review and bilingual synchronization needed before maintenance is complete. Retained failures and unmodeled claims do not require inventing successful proofs.
