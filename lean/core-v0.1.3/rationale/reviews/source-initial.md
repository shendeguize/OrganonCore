## Initial judgment and expected fidelity boundaries

The source supports a limited formal interpretation of selected responsibility and support relations, if the interpretation explicitly preserves normative force, applicability, and the distinction between supporting a claim and examining its support. It does not supply a complete formal ontology, inference calculus, support metric, or source-to-model correspondence proof. The success of a Lean check could establish an encoded statement under its assumptions; its relevance to the prose remains a separate judgment. Unknown correspondence is not a pass, a core conflict, or proof that the source is defective.

1. Core preamble: adopted commitments, meanings, and application limits constitute the authority; they are not descriptive laws of every system. Charter and Grounds mutually constrain each other without priority or deductive hierarchy. Rationale and contextual skill requirements must retain their separate status.
2. Self-Transcendence: expansion is valued, not merely allowed. Organization, methods and principles remain revisable. Orientation, permission, actual change and supported achievement are different. Universal nonfinality within the relevant existing forms does not impose change on every action, guarantee progress, exclude justified stability, or demand independence from external help.
3. Internal Consistency: preserve simultaneous holding and implications, same question, assumptions, meanings and scope. Changing or withdrawing a judgment may remove co-holding; concealed redefinition cannot erase a prior contradiction. Tension is not yet contradiction. Compatibility cannot stand for correctness, adequacy or inferential entailment.
4. Reflexivity: both system and formation/application/revision of principles are assessment and generation objects, subject to their applicable conditions. No self exemption when conditions hold, no unconditional application of every principle to itself. Self-application and internally produced revision do not establish correctness or sufficient grounds.
5. Grounds and Assessment: articulability identifies concepts, assumptions, reasons and limits. Assessment examines whether those grounds support the corresponding claim; completing it may establish insufficient support. It must not be encoded as an obligation to obtain a successful support verdict, nor as a completed marker lacking claim/grounds/conditions correspondence. Each applicable mixed aspect retains its responsibility even without a classification tag. Empirical assessment examines evidence/performance plus support conditions, scope and uncertainty; inferential assessment examines assumptions and inferential relations; value assessment states position, reasons, limits and consequences and stays open to relevant criticism. An initial value commitment need not prove every starting assumption. The general proportionate-support constraint limits claims; it does not guarantee all assessed claims are supported.
6. Scope and tools: local agreement cannot establish unconditional universals or absence of omitted differences. Measurement, repetition and frameworks can be useful but form no universally mandatory chain. A verifiable unreproduced observation may offer limited support; stochastic output identity and stability of conclusions are different.
7. Capability claims: application-specific definitions and conditions determine the actual claim. External evidence may support a capability without internal explanation; reliable output does not automatically establish understanding. An application may explicitly require explanation. Counts alone do not establish expansion. Narrowing a definition creates a new claim and cannot retroactively prove the old one.
8. Choice commitment: relevant reasons connect priority to objectives, values and constraints. Name, convention or status alone are insufficient; tradition may embody an explicitly adopted value with a relevant connection. This additional commitment is not derived merely from grouping it under Grounds. Openness does not imply equivalence, multiple feasible means, mandatory novelty, or rejection of established methods.
